# Food and Diet

**The short version.** Dan talks about food constantly and has never once
recorded an opinion about how it tastes. He has no favourite dish and no ranking
of any kind. What he has instead is a strict rule about **what is allowed to be
in it** — and that rule is absolute.

## Food is everywhere in his life except as taste

It turns up as work: three jobs, one of them forty-one months inside a single
cuisine. As harm: an eating disorder as a teenager, candy as a staple, two teeth
lost. As a regional joke retold for thirty years. As a figure of speech. It is
even tattooed on his arm.

**What it never is, anywhere in the records, is something he has a view about.**

That's strange, because he is famously opinionated. Everything else in his
life — music, language, politics, film, work, even strangers — gets run through
one harsh filter that sorts the genuine from the fake. Food is the one thing he
deals with constantly that the filter has never been pointed at.

## What replaced the taste opinions

An earlier version of this page said food was missing from his judgements
entirely. That turned out to be too strong, and what replaced it is more
interesting.

He describes eating *"quite literally, like a toddler"* — chicken strips, french
fries, candy and fruit as staples. But the staples aren't the finding. The
finding is what he calls *"an absolute, dealbreaking, no-questions-asked
policy"* about everything else:

| Item | What is allowed to be in it |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cheeseburger | meat, cheese, bread |
| Taco | meat, cheese, shell |
| Sandwich | meat, cheese, roll |
| Vegetables accepted | corn, lettuce — and nothing else |
| Refused outright | anything with onion or tomato |
| Absolute exclusion | pickles, *"the mortal enemy"* |

Look at the middle column again. Every item is a normal food **with its
additions stripped out**, cut back to only the parts he named. Nothing is ranked
against anything else. The rule isn't about which foods are good. It's about
what is permitted to be present — and the answer is: only what he asked for.

**The refusal list isn't random.** Corn and lettuce sit there separately without
changing anything, and they can be picked off. Onion, tomato and pickle all
**soak in.** They wet the bread and flavour whatever they touch, and once
they've done it you can't undo it. The vegetables he accepts are the removable
ones. The ones he refuses have already committed the food to something before it
reaches him.

## This is his general habit, applied to a plate

Almost everything in Dan's life gets sorted by one question: *did I choose this,
or was it handed to me?* Food looked for a long time like the one big exception.
It isn't. It's where that sorting is enforced most strictly and most physically.

A sandwich that arrives carrying something he didn't ask for is an object with an
unrequested part in it. It gets refused outright rather than negotiated — the
same move he makes on inherited culture and on claims from people above him, run
on a plate, in about a second, with no argument.

The distinction the older version missed is between **judging** and
**specifying**. He doesn't judge food. He specifies it, and enforcement is
total. Those are two different mental habits, and only the first one leaves
behind a list of favourites.

## Why it's all-or-nothing rather than a strong preference

Across eight other areas of his life, questions that call for a verdict — is
this any good, can I trust this, is this legitimate — collapse into exactly one
of two answers with nothing in the middle. Food is the ninth, and the most
literal.

*"The mortal enemy"* and *"an absolute, dealbreaking, no-questions-asked policy"*
aren't just strong words. They're the vocabulary of a decision with no third
option. There's no case on record of an onion tolerated in small quantity, or
picked off and the food eaten anyway. Every ingredient is accepted completely or
refused completely.

His cognitive profile explains the shape. His dominant mode is internal logical
analysis, measured at **96th percentile**, and it runs a pass/fail test — does
this hold together, not how much of it holds together. The function that would
normally produce a softer answer like *"fine, in a small amount"* is social and
relational, and it measures **10th percentile** — the weakest of his five. So a
sandwich with something unrequested on it fails the test outright, and there's no
negotiating function available to talk it down to "good enough."

A plate is the cleanest possible place to see this, because a sandwich can't
argue back, charm him, or be given the benefit of the doubt the way a person
sometimes can.

## The numbers that make this a finding rather than a gap

"No food opinions on record" would mean nothing if he simply didn't eat or
didn't write things down. Both are ruled out.

| What was checked | What came back | What it rules out |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| His curated favourites list | **2,016 entries in exactly four categories — Music 1,860, Book 120, Art 25, Movie 11. Zero food.** | That he doesn't rank things. He does, 2,016 times. |
| Word counts across his message archive (29 MB, about 217k rows) | `food` **968** · `eat` **650** · `hungry` **237** · `pizza` **189** · `restaurant` **101** | That food is missing from daily life. It beats `album` (47), `song` (96) and `track` (108). |
| Judgement phrasing — "best," "worst," "favorite" next to a food word | **6 hits in total.** "Favorite food" returns **0**. | That the opinions existed and just never got filed. |

The middle row is the one that settles it. He talks about food **more** than he
talks about music, in the same archive, in the same years — and music produced
1,860 recorded verdicts while food produced none. This isn't a subject nobody
watched. It's a subject nobody judged.

## The five jobs food actually does in his life

**Work.** A Midtown Lebanese restaurant from 2021 to 2024 is the strongest test
available: forty-one months as a busser and then a server, exposed every day to
tableside shawarma and specific wines. What the records show is genuine affection
for the Lebanese culture and the humour of the place — including the night the
owners comped his mother's entire meal. The culture registered. The food didn't.
A year of courier work in 2019–2020 produced **290 messages**, every single one
an automated dispatch alert, none about what was in the bag. A gas-station job in
2026 is food handed across a counter, not chosen.

**Harm.** The only place food is a subject in its own right is where it did
damage: *"bulimic as a high schooler,"* candy as a diet staple, and the adult
result of **two lost teeth, one of them a visible canine.**

**Region.** An Eat'n Park *Frownie* cookie — the frowning version of the chain's
Smiley Cookie — brought to the family after a 1998 funeral, written up in an
essay by his relative Alex Frank, and still quoted by Dan nearly three decades
later. It's one of the most durable food references he has, and it isn't about a
cookie. It's about small-town Western Pennsylvania producing a perfectly timed
dark joke at a funeral.

**Figure of speech.** "Diet" survives mostly in its borrowed sense — *media
diet*, *information diet*. The word outlived the thing it names.

**Tattoo.** A Misfits skull inside a pizza slice, on his forearm, done **13
October 2018** at roughly **3 inches by 1.5 inches**. His stated reasoning, in a
November 2018 exchange, is entirely about the band: he didn't want the standard
version *"where it looks like it's in a coffin with the arms folded,"* found a
pizza-slice reinterpretation instead, and had it drawn custom. He has pizza on
his arm for life and it is a Misfits tattoo.

## The reason is written down, not guessed

This doesn't have to be inferred, because he stated it himself. The framework he
wrote to govern how AI assistants work with his records contains the instruction
**"the food and the cat are always real"** — meaning food and the animals are
exempt from the forensic treatment everything else gets.

That's the whole finding in his own words. The same machinery that produced
inequality statistics, message-timing analysis and a decade-long verdict on a
relationship is explicitly told to leave food alone, because food is the thing
that stays real while everything else is being taken apart. **Food isn't
unjudged by accident. It's protected.**

## The honest complication

There's a genuine disagreement between this page and another. The entry on his
master filter says music, language, politics, film, work and strangers "all go
through the same gate." Food doesn't — and food is mentioned more often than
music is.

The resolution isn't that the filter has an exception. It's that the filter has a
**territory.** One high-contact area it never enters at all, by an exemption he
wrote down himself. "One standard for everything" should be read as "one standard
for everything it's allowed to touch."

## What would prove this wrong

- **A food opinion that is genuinely about flavour.** If one ever surfaces, this
  page predicts it will sort on region, class or authenticity — the usual filter
  wearing a food costume, like the tattoo. A recorded preference framed purely as
  *this tastes better* is the cleanest available falsifier.
- **A food entry appearing in the favourites list.** It has held four categories
  across 2,016 entries. A fifth would show the gap was just a filing habit rather
  than a real protection.
- **Restaurant or cooking material that reads as taste.** Any future material
  should read as logistics or as labour, matching the courier and restaurant
  shape. If it reads as appreciation, the rule is in trouble.

**One prediction has already been scored, and it was wrong.** The page predicted
that any food judgement surfacing would sort on region, class or authenticity. It
sorts on **composition** instead — which is the same underlying instinct applied
to an object's parts rather than its origin. Directionally right, mechanically
wrong. Recorded here rather than quietly corrected.

## What we still don't know

- **The word counts are crude.** `food` at 968 counts every use of the word, and
  the six judgement hits came from a fixed list of phrases. A proper pass would
  hand-code a sample rather than trust the pattern.
- **The message archive stops around August 2025.** So the "no evaluation" claim
  is strongest for 2015 to 2025 and weakest for the most recent year, including
  the whole gas-station period.
- **Nothing here covers what he says out loud or on the phone.** That's invisible
  to a text archive, and it's exactly where food opinions would normally live.
  This is the most serious limit on the page, and it can't be fixed from the
  records that exist.
- **Nobody knows when the eating rule started.** Whether it came before or after
  the teenage eating disorder and the candy period is unknown — and the answer
  decides whether this is a control habit that grew out of a disordered one, or
  an independent lifelong trait.
- **Cooking ability, and real allergies as distinct from refusals, are
  unrecorded.** So is whether the rule has changed over the years; the account of
  it is written in the present tense and dates nothing.
