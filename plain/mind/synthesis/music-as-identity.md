# Music as Identity

**The short version.** Music is not a hobby in this life. It is not a "top artists" list or a collection of favorites filed by genre. It is one of the few stable structures of self that has run the entire length of the documented life — as a teenage identity performed in public, as a literal itinerary of tour dates and concert halls, as a private emotional regulator, and as a professional identity spanning four aliases and over a decade of production. The old way of filing each artist as a one-line favorites entry systematically under-reported this, because the meaning was never in any single artist. The meaning was in the *through-line*. Music is the spine that connects the teenage Facebook statuses, the tour-following years, the Elliott Smith obsession, the producer aliases, and the way he falls in love with a band and then refuses to follow it past a self-set boundary. Each of those is a facet. This page states the whole.

## The four modes music has actually served

### 1. Public self, performed

The 2007 Facebook statuses are a self presented to the world as a coordinated aesthetic stance. "Swooning over the new FOB." "Say Anything obsession." "Slaying on halo." "Super Mario Galaxy." Music and gaming were part of the mask — not behind it. They were the identity performed outward for an audience, the aesthetic position staked in real time on a social platform where everyone could see it.

This is not the same as private listening or deep emotional attachment. This is the *use* of music as social signal. The 2007 Facebook layer is a record of a person who understood that what you publicly love says something about who you are — and who chose to make that statement loudly. The music was not just something he enjoyed. It was something he *performed* as part of a public identity, a way of telling the world: this is who I am, this is what I care about, this is the aesthetic I belong to.

### 2. Itinerary and devotion

The mid-2000s scene was not something Dan watched from a distance. He toured it. Three Fall Out Boy shows in eleven months. A release-day TRL taping. A Say Anything date in Baltimore. Bamboozle at the Meadowlands. This is fandom as logistics — the kind of devotion that requires planning, travel, time, and money. It is not passive consumption. It is a lifestyle organized around the schedule of other people's art.

The secret-show alias "Schrute Farms" is the detail that proves he was let in on the in-joke — not watching from outside it. He was inside the scene enough to have a code name for something the artists themselves created. That alias is not just a funny name. It is evidence of belonging — of being close enough to the source to receive the signal and understand the reference.

### 3. Emotional regulator

Elliott Smith is the number one individual artist in a library otherwise built on bass-heavy electronic warfare. The "quietest, most intimate presence." This is the pole that proves the emotional range extends to its opposite — that the person who produces sub-bass-dominant electronic music also sits with Elliott Smith in private, and that the Smith pole is not a minor exception but the primary one.

The 2025 "R&B before 1979 or it's trash… go back to Elliott Smith" gate shows the regulation is still live and still enforced. The Elliott Smith line is not a nostalgic memory. It is a standing aesthetic gate that operates in the present tense. When the world gets too loud, the regulation kicks in, and the answer is the quietest, most intimate artist in the library. That is not a preference. It is a mechanism.

### 4. Professional identity

Since roughly 2013, Dan has been a producer under four aliases: SLOPPP, then MOGZART, then MOGGED-UP, then GRIPNOTIC. All sub-bass-dominant. All unified by an involuntary low-frequency signature that was identified post-hoc, not set as a deliberate aesthetic goal. The move to electronic production was not a retreat from rock — it was a correct forward read of where authentic expression lived after rock's 1963 chart peak. Production is the architecture. He is a producer-first identity, not a performer.

The four aliases are not four different aesthetic visions. They are four names for the same underlying signature, which means the aliases mark periods of a life rather than changes of artistic direction. The sub-bass thread runs through all of them. The aliases changed. The genre targets shifted. The signature did not move.

## The cutoff as a personality signature

The affection has edges, and the edges reveal the mechanism.

Fall Out Boy love stops at *Folie à Deux* (2008) and does not extend to the post-hiatus reunion band. Say Anything and the emo cluster are claimed up to the 2010 imprinting window and then frozen. The pattern is consistent: intense, whole-catalog engagement with a thing, followed by a hard refusal to follow it past a self-set boundary. The pre-cut version is held as the "real" one. Everything that came after is not rejected because it is bad — it is rejected because it came after the boundary.

This pattern mirrors the relationship architecture documented elsewhere in the record: maximum absorption, then a clean cutoff, with the pre-cut version held as the real one. Music preference is where this shows up cleanest, because the objects cannot betray him. A band can release an album he doesn't like. A person can do something much worse. The music version of the pattern is pure, because the stakes are aesthetic rather than relational.

## Why a list was never enough

The old favorites entries treated music as a catalog: artist name, one line, genre tag. That format captured none of the actual structure. It missed that Elliott Smith is not one item on a list but the emotional pole of a whole identity. It missed that Fall Out Boy is not a current favorite but a sealed time capsule, frozen at a specific album and guarded by a boundary that has held for over fifteen years. It missed that the four aliases are not four different projects but one signature wearing four names.

Music is a primary, century-spanning structure of self in this life. Not a list. Each linked page carries one facet. This one states the whole.

## What we still don't know

- **The 2019–2024 message gap means the live concert habit after 2008 is under-documented.** Only the 2005–2008 record survives in Dan's own hand. Whether the tour-following lifestyle continued into the late 2010s and early 2020s is not settled by the record.
- **Whether the producer identity and the favorites pole ever consciously cross.** Does Dan hear his own sub-bass signature in what he consumes? Does the Elliott Smith private pole ever intersect with the GRIPNOTIC professional output? The corpus does not settle this.
