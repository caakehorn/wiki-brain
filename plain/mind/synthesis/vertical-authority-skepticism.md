# Vertical Authority Skepticism

**The short version.** Dan treats anyone *above* him — fathers, managers,
owners, institutions, the government — as suspect by default, and gives trust
only to a small number of people *alongside* him. This isn't a political
opinion he argues for. It shows up in personality-test scores, in his work history,
in his politics, and in social media posts going back to 2007.

## Two axes

Think of two directions.

**Vertical** is anyone with authority over him. **Horizontal** — or lateral — is
a peer, someone on his level.

His personality test scores put numbers on it: Trust scores 9 out of 100.
Altruism 1. Submissiveness 1. Assertiveness 5.

That last number is the interesting one. Low assertiveness means he isn't trying
to climb the ladder or take it over — if he were, assertiveness would be high.
He doesn't obey the hierarchy and he doesn't compete for the top of it. He steps
off the ladder entirely and inspects it from outside.

## Where it comes from

There are two candidate origins, and they teach different lessons.

**November 2005.** Dan's father went into rehab for cocaine, his mother's affair
with the dealer came out, and the marriage ended. The lesson: a trusted
authority figure can be running an entirely hidden second reality.

**Earlier: childhood.** Dan described this himself in 2026. His father had a
pattern of publicly confronting and humiliating him — pulling him out of rooms
at parties, stores and family gatherings to scream at him where everyone who'd
just watched him leave could still hear.

The clearest example: his father found a harmless website in the family browser
history, decided on his own that it broke "the spirit" of the household rules,
and enforced that by confronting Dan's friend and humiliating Dan in front of
him.

That second origin teaches something different from the first. 2005 teaches that
an authority figure might be *lying*. The childhood pattern teaches that an
authority figure's idea of what even counts as an offence is unpredictable, and
that punishment happens in public. That's arguably a more direct template for
distrusting authority *itself*, rather than distrusting one particular person's
honesty. Dan's aunt Wendy — his father's sister — independently arrived at the
same theory about why Dan avoids being around him now.

Even the positive figure in his childhood fits the pattern. His
great-grandmother Fran, whom he calls his biggest influence, treated him as a
peer and a patron rather than as an authority.

## It shows up at every scale

**At work.** The earliest well-documented case is a company he worked for in
2011–12. His final paycheck was held up for six weeks; the company's accountant
wrote to him, "It's a trust issue. We don't trust you." Dan filed complaints
with the IRS and the New York State Department of Labor. Four months later he
disclosed that the company president had used Dan's own account to pressure
former employees and had altered departing interns' contracts to keep them
quiet. The president retaliated by filing a notarised statement claiming Dan had
never worked there at all.

The cleanest small example came fourteen years later: an assistant manager
demanded $50 off the books, claiming a till was short. Dan read it immediately
as an unverified claim from above whose *missing paperwork was the whole point*,
and answered with refusal and documentation instead of paying.

The record is honest about the other side of this. At a restaurant job lasting
41 months, staff were convinced the owners were skimming the tip pool and Dan ran
what he called a "decentralized audit" — and the same record documents him
sabotaging that employer himself.

**At the level of the state.** His analysis of January 6th distrusts every
authority in the story simultaneously — the intelligence agencies, the Capitol
Police, and both political parties — and lands on "everyone is in the wrong, now
let's score who is *more* wrong." No team. Underneath all that theory sits
something concrete: a 2015 arrest for possession, the earliest record of Dan on
the receiving end of government authority rather than analysing it from a
distance.

**Online, earliest of all.** His Facebook archive shows the instinct before he
had any politics to attach it to: groups devoted to spotting scams in 2007 ("this
is some sort of scam. there is NO way i got this wrong"), a "patron saint of
liars and fakes" persona, and tracked interests in Elections, Congress, the
Constitution and Law. The detective instinct came first. The ideology found it
later.

## The exception, and what it really is

Trust does get extended sideways, to very few people. Two are named in the
records: Tom, his closest male friend, and Jerad, his first call on politics.

But even they get audited. After a supply failure in May 2026, Dan revised his
model of Tom to "reliable ally only when our interests line up; unreliable when
they don't" — without ending the friendship.

That leads to the sharpest correction on this page. **Peers are not trusted more
than authorities. They are audited later.**

Look at the timing. The $50 till claim was taken apart the same day. A
manipulative colleague's blame-shifting pattern was mapped *before* it reached
him. AI models get anti-softening instructions installed before they've produced
a single word to object to. Against all that: the friendship with Tom dates to
the late 2000s and was reviewed for the first time in 2026, *after* the failure
rather than before it.

So the exception is real, but it's an exception in **timing**, not in whether
scrutiny eventually arrives.

That turns a personality trait into a prediction: a newly trusted friend should
show no investigative activity at all until something goes wrong — and the delay
should be longer the more Dan is depending on them.

## What it buys and what it costs

**It works.** The till contradiction was caught in real time. The colleague's
blame-shifting was mapped before it landed on him.

**It costs.** There is career friction at every single job in the record. The
assessments state flatly that he is unsuited to conventional employment —
"bureaucracy is systemic death." And there's the loneliness of holding positions
no group will sign on to.

Music is the one area with no authority in it at all: self-released,
self-owned, no manager, no label. The records connect that directly to its being
the one channel where he is sincere.

## What we still don't know

- **There is no case anywhere in the record of an institution earning trust
  back.** The model has no path to rehabilitation — nothing has ever moved from
  the suspect column to the trusted one. Whether that's because it can't happen
  or just hasn't is unknown.
- **There's a gap in the records for 2021–23**, so the relationship with the
  restaurant employer during those years is reconstructed only from how Dan
  described it later, not from anything written at the time.
