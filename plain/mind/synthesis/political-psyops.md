# Political Psyops & Hybrid Events

**The short version.** Dan is a democratic socialist. He has no loyalty to Trump
or to the right. And he has spent years building a case that January 6th was
partly a staged event. This entry is about **what he believes and how he
reasons** — not about whether he is right.

## What he claims, and what he does not

The claim is narrow. It is easy to get wrong.

He does **not** say the rally was fake. He says a small operation rode on top of
a real riot. Its purpose was to create an image: a martyr for one side, and
revolutionary violence for the other. The point was to damage the populist
right's chances at future elections.

He calls this a **hybrid** event. Real riot, inserted core. He treats the
question as settled.

## The four things he points at

He built the case by studying raw footage. It falls into four groups.

**The shooting itself.** Ashli Babbitt was shot in the torso. The bullet did not
exit. Blood pooled inside her rather than spraying, which he calls the difference
between real pathology and "Hollywood physics." He reads that as a real wound,
but not the one first described. The officer who fired was not named publicly for
months.

**Police procedure.** Nobody set up a perimeter after a person was shot dead in a
volatile crowd. The medical response looks token to him — someone unzipped a
jacket. Officers mixed into the crowd instead of holding it back. SWAT stepped
aside at the key doorway.

**How the crowd behaved.** There was an open door a few feet away, and the crowd
ignored it. Senator Romney escaped through that door. The fighting in the footage
looks to him like "a sad mosh pit." Police handcuffed the man who smashed the
window, then let him go.

**Who was filming.** The only clean video of the shooting came from John
Sullivan. He is an anti-Trump activist, and he was standing in exactly the right
place.

## He gave it a name

In a session on **2 January 2026**, Dan worked this up into a named theory with
an AI assistant. He called it **"Operation Wildfall."**

The structure he calls a **"Sandwich."** A real outer layer, which is the riot.
An inserted core, which is the shooting. He calls the shooting a **"Psy-Kill"** —
the thing that turns a messy crowd into a branded terrorist attack.

He says Babbitt was chosen for effect. In his words, *"she was a prop"* —
unarmed, small, white, a female Air Force veteran. He calls the wider plan
**"Inverted Containment."** Leave the outside loose so people get in. Give up the
inside so the pictures come out clean.

The timestamps show him asking to be checked, not agreed with. At 10:32 PM he
asked for *"a FAIR and HONEST assessment."* At 11:00 PM he asked it to *"identify
any glaring … factual errors."*

**The theory is older than that night.** A ChatGPT session from **15 June 2025**
has him asking the same question, seven months earlier, on a different platform.
He reached the same verdict: a real shooting, which he put at about **95%**, with
the story weaponised rather than invented. That he landed twice in the same place
shows how settled it already was.

## What the machines said back

This is the part that makes the record worth reading. The AI answers were not
simply agreeable.

One split the difference. It said much of his supporting detail was **a Gish
Gallop** — a flood of weak claims that only looks strong in bulk. It said a lot
of the odd behaviour on camera was real trauma. People freeze under stress.

It allowed only three things as real flags: the framing was unusually perfect,
the procedural breaches were textbook, and the footage appeared very fast.

Dan's reply is the sharpest line in the file. It is also a concession. **"The
point was the imagery; the execution didn't need to be perfect."** Almost nobody
watches raw footage. Calling something fringe is enough to keep people from
looking.

A second model started out skeptical and moved partway. It agreed a small group
would not face a big coordination problem. It called the procedural gaps his
strongest point. It rated the whole thing "the most internally coherent version
of J6 revisionism" it had seen. It settled on saying the event "warrants genuine
forensic investigation that has not occurred."

That is not agreement that he is right. It is agreement that the questions exist.

## The documents — and a warning about them

Later in the same chat, Dan uploaded real documents. He asked the model to read
each one against his theory. They included a records release on the shooting, a
Park Police planning file, the public timeline, and part of the January 6
Committee's final report.

**Treat this whole section as an AI's summary of a document, not as checked
fact.** Those files are not held in these archives. Unlike the message records,
nobody has compared them to the originals.

**On the shooting file.** The official papers put the wound in four different
places. The Death Report says *"upper portion of the left chest near the
clavicle."* The autopsy summary says *"left anterior shoulder."* A prosecutor's
email that day says *"left shoulder."* A witness sergeant says *"the chest
somewhere."* The model's comment was blunt. This is *"not exculpatory for the
official account — that's a problem for it."* Nobody can argue about the bullet's
path when the starting point keeps moving. The same file shows Capitol Police
lawyers asking for radio recordings back, then never sending them to
investigators. It also states that Capitol Police wore no body cameras. So
civilian phone video is the only footage that exists.

**On the Park Police file.** An alert at **11:19 AM** put more than **200 Proud
Boys** on Capitol grounds. Trump was still speaking at the Ellipse, and he did not
finish until about **1:10 PM**. An FBI report noted people already under
investigation in the crowd, with their status blacked out. The Secret Service
logged people at the Ellipse checkpoints in helmets and body armour, carrying
military radios. The model's summary named the gap. A large security force went
to the Ellipse. The Capitol got much less, even though intelligence already
placed an organised group there.

**On the Committee report.** Three details it called underexamined. Two days
before the event, on **4 January**, the Defense Secretary barred the DC National
Guard from bringing weapons, helmets, body armour or riot gear without his
personal say-so. Three days before, the Capitol Police Board decided not to ask
for the Guard at all. And the Army denied for two weeks that Lt. Gen. Charles
Flynn — brother of Michael Flynn — was on the **2:26 PM** call where the Guard
was held up. The timeline later confirmed he was.

The model's own closing line is the fairest summary of the section. This is **a
cumulative pattern, not a smoking gun.** A security system got clear warnings,
sent too few people, took away their gear, added layers of sign-off, and then
investigated each failure separately so no overall picture formed.

## The wider politics

January 6th is not a one-off for him. It fits a bigger story about rot.

He sees the Trump era as a zero-sum team sport run on one man's short-term
interest. He sees the Democratic Party as bought, too old at the top, and hostile
to economic populists. Sanders in 2016 was the chance that got away. 2020 plus
Covid is where he gave up. He sets identity-focused liberalism against a
class-focused left, and reads Chinese success against American self-harm.

His own path is documented. Raised Republican. Out by 2006. Hard left by 2019,
via Bernie Sanders and the Chapo Trap House podcast. The turn even has a date —
**22 August 2020** — which he narrated in his own messages at the time.

**One caution against over-explaining him.** His two closest political friends,
Jerad Friedline and Josh Brannan, both grew up with him in Uniontown. Both started
from the same Bernie-aligned place. Both ended up with the same reluctant
fascination with Trump. Three people from one small circle landing in the same
spot points at a wider pattern. This looks like a Rust Belt millennial cohort
soured by how the Democratic establishment treated Sanders. It is not a personal
quirk that needs its own explanation. He just documented it in more detail than
most.

need **every part to be innocent at once**, treat that as a signal. He runs the
same check on a workplace dispute over a till, on relationships, and on his own
AI work.

## Politics as entertainment

A second track runs alongside the forensic one. They do not compete.

In his own words, politics is *"primary entertainment + near-total ideological
pursuit."* It is about spectacle. As he put it: *"i can't tell you how much fun
i'm having with it."* He fed the whole Trump era into an AI as raw material for a
"Post-Pax Americana" epic he wanted to write.

His reading matches. His book favourites count **79 on politics and 69 on
history.**

That double register is the key to the whole page. It is what lets a committed
socialist pick apart a right-coded theory with no stake in the outcome. The
spectacle is just a text. He reads it with the same tools he uses on music trends
or office politics.

## What would prove this wrong

The page is honest about its weak points. They are worth stating plainly.

- **The document section is unchecked.** All of it is an AI's summary of files
  nobody has compared to the originals. One bad reading would take a large part
  of the case with it.
- **He agrees much of his own evidence is weak.** He accepted the Gish Gallop
  verdict rather than fighting it. A stack of weak claims does not get strong by
  growing. Only three flags survived.
- **Innocent explanations cover a lot of it.** People freeze under stress. That
  accounts for a real share of the strange footage, and it was granted rather
  than waved away.
- **By its own admission there is no smoking gun.** The best version of the
  argument is a pattern of failures. But that is also what plain incompetence
  looks like. The case cannot tell the two apart, and does not claim to.
- **The conclusion goes nowhere.** He reconstructs an institutional failure in
  detail, reaches a confident verdict, and then does nothing with it. The
  analysis is the finished product, not a step toward one.
