# The Configured Body

**The short version.** Dan treats his body like a machine, not like a body. He
controls what goes into it with extraordinary strictness, and he watches how it
looks to other people, constantly and painfully. What's missing is the thing in
between — **upkeep.** Nothing gets maintained or repaired. Damage just
accumulates until it shows.

## Two settings, and no third

He **specifies the inputs** — down to which vegetables are allowed to touch a
sandwich, and which milligram of which drug holds the floor. He **monitors the
outputs** — how the result reads to other people, without pause.

What is absent from a seventeen-year, **217,573-message** record is
**maintenance.**

This isn't the ordinary neglect of somebody who never thinks about his body. His
body is one of the most intensively managed things in the entire archive. It's
managed the way you'd manage a piece of equipment: set up carefully at the input,
read carefully at the output, and never serviced.

**The two lost teeth are what that costs.** By 2025 a visible canine is gone, and
he gives his own explanation for it — *"bulimic as a high schooler candy as a
staple of my diet terrified of dentist (aka haven't gone) suboxone cigarettes."*
Every item on that list is either an input he chose or an output he watched. Not
one is something he stopped doing to himself. The damage is what piles up in the
gap between the two settings that are running.

## Setting one: specifying what goes in

This is the best-documented thing in his health records, and the surprise is that
it works identically for drugs and for food. The records had been treating those
as two separate subjects.

| Area | The specification | How it's enforced |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Opioid floor | Suboxone daily since 2010-02-17, 1–2 mg, sixteen years, zero relapses | Absolute — the one regime never interrupted |
| Stimulants | Cocaine, which he calls "CPU & RAM," dosed as high as money allows | Continuous |
| Alcohol | **Zero**, for thirteen to fifteen years, with active aversion | Total exclusion, no exceptions on record |
| Heroin | Tried once, winter 2008, refused — *"felt dirty"* | Refused on how it **felt**, not on health |
| A meal | Cheeseburger = meat, cheese, bread. Corn and lettuce allowed; no other vegetable | *"An absolute, dealbreaking, no-questions-asked policy"* |
| Onion, tomato, pickle | Refused outright — the three that soak in and can't be picked off later | Refused before the plate arrives |

Read the alcohol row against the heroin row and the whole thing gives itself
away. **Neither refusal is a health decision.** Alcohol is out because it's the
substance tied to how his family betrayed him — there are four functional
alcoholics across three generations behind that. Heroin was refused because of
how it felt to take. Somebody keeping his body healthy would not produce that
pair. Somebody *composing* a body would produce exactly it.

The food rule turns out to be the same operation in different clothing: not a
ranking of what's good, but a total rule about what's allowed to be present —
with the refusal list consisting precisely of the things you can't remove
afterwards. That's the same shape as a fifteen-year single-drug floor with an
absolute exclusion bolted on. **Judging and specifying are different mental
habits, and this body only runs the second one.**

## Setting two: watching how it looks

The monitoring is just as well documented and points somewhere else entirely —
not at what the body contains, but at **how it reads from outside**.

He describes it himself as *"the anxious, internally probing awareness of my
appearance in all senses,"* and the loop is self-feeding: the watching generates
the very problems it's watching for. His self-consciousness measures at the
**91st percentile**.

Tellingly, when the lost tooth came up in 2025 he scored it as an appearance
event, not a medical one — *"the additional self-consious, humiliating image
issue."*

Watching produces information and no action. That's not a criticism, it's the
structural point. A monitoring system with nothing attached to fix what it finds
can only escalate, because every reading adds to the pile and nothing clears it.

## The missing setting, actually counted

Other records asserted flatly that there was *"no labs, no dental, no primary
care anywhere in 17 years of messages"* — with no count behind it. Counted, that
turns out to be **too strong, and the corrected version is more interesting.**

| :--- | ---: | ---: |

**Dental care does exist in the record, and his own account of it is wrong.** His
2025 explanation for the lost teeth says *"terrified of dentist (aka haven't
gone)."* The messages contradict that directly:

- **2017-10-09:** *"went back to the dentist today and i've got a lot of work to
  get done. all those years of being a delinquent junkie catching up to me."*
- **2017-10-26:** *"I've got a dentist appt at 830."*
- **2017-11-09:** asking for a ride to the dentist at 3:30.
- **2017-11-30:** *"When I went back for a 1wk checkup on the second, they
  noticed a hole that hadn't yet begun to develop tissue so they put in a
  membrane and restitched it"* — a post-surgical follow-up, meaning there was a
  graft.
- **2020-09-05:** *"setting up a dentist appointment asap."*
- **2024-07-13:** *"I have a dentist appointment and work on the same day."*

There was a full dental episode in autumn 2017, including surgery and a follow-up
he kept. When the written record and his later memory disagree, the record wins
and the disagreement gets logged rather than quietly smoothed over. **What
survives is a better claim: the upkeep is occasional and reactive, not absent.**
He goes when something has already broken — which is why the 2017 visit opens
with *"a lot of work to get done"* instead of with a cleaning.

Three more things the count settles.

**There is a prescribing doctor, and the entire relationship is paperwork.** He
had previously declined to say whether the Suboxone was prescribed — *"no
comment"* was his one hard line on it. The 2025 messages answer it. **2025-03-06:**
*"I had my doctor move my prescription here."* **2025-06-08:** *"my doctor said
the pharmacy won't fill an out of state prescription."* **2025-06-12:** *"my
doctor tried to send a prescription to walmart and they won't fill it either so
i'm completely out of options now."* And six years earlier, **2019-05-31:** *"try
to go get subs from my old doctor."*

So it's prescribed. But every single recorded exchange with that doctor is about
moving a script between pharmacies. None is about a dose, a taper, a review, or
his body. **The one standing medical relationship in his life is a logistics
relationship** — which is exactly what you'd predict from a system that has an
input-supply function and no maintenance function.

**It flips the supply picture around.** Other records treat his friend Tom as the
standing supply line for Suboxone. The June 2025 sequence runs the other way: the
prescription fails at Walmart on the 12th, and on the 13th — *"I am so fucked.
I'm gonna have to drive down to see Tom today"* — Tom is the backup. So the
default route is the prescription and the dealer network is the fallback, which
makes the May 2026 collapse a **second** failure with the first line already
down, not a single point of failure giving way.

**The barrier he names is money, not fear.** Every sentence about his own care is
hedged — *"I know you rly don't want me to go to a doctor but I'm telling you I
should go"* (2017-09-28), *"i should get into a doctor for that before caddie
season ends"* (2017-10-08), *"I might need to go see a doctor if my voice doesn't
start coming back"* (2018-07-13), *"I'm probably going to need to go to the
doctor"* (2018-07-15). None is followed by an actual visit anywhere in the
record. Alongside them: *"The copay for the doctor would be about that much"*
(2017-06-24), and in February 2020, *"imagine being able to just go to the doctor
or dentist when you need"* and *"the doctor thing is a really big thing for
me...like, there's no reason that 50,000 people die a year because they don't
have insurance."*

## The comparison that gives the game away

Now set those hedged sentences about himself against how he talks about other
people's bodies — same years, same archive.

| About himself | About someone else |
| :--- | :--- |
| *"I'm telling you I should go"* (2017-09-28) | *"I kept telling her to make an appointment with a doctor"* (2017-06-24, about his great-grandmother) |
| *"i should get into a doctor for that before caddie season ends"* (2017-10-08) | *"I wish she would have just gone to see her doctor months ago when I told her to"* (2017-06-25) |
| *"I might need to go see a doctor"* (2018-07-13) | *"if you are legitimately unable to not SHIT YOUR PANTS then go to a fucking doctor"* (2018-10-11) |
| *"I'm probably going to need to go to the doctor"* (2018-07-15) | *"I just ran out of the apartment to get him to a doctor asap"* (2023-10-29, about the cat) |

**Every sentence about himself is conditional. Every sentence about someone else
is a command**, and several are urgent enough to be rude. Same person, same
years, same subject. Four months separate *"I wish she would have just gone to
see her doctor months ago"* from *"i should get into a doctor for that before
caddie season ends."*

This is his general habit of splitting the world in two, showing up inside a
single body. He normally sorts things by where they came from: things imposed on
him get inspected immediately, things he chose himself get left alone until they
break. A body isn't obviously either one. What the evidence shows is that **the
body gets split down the middle rather than filed on one side.** The *setup* —
the drug stack, the exclusion list, the meal rule — is his own choice and gets the
treatment reserved for things imposed on him: checked instantly, absolutely, with
no argument. The *substrate* — the teeth, the lungs, the actual flesh the setup
runs on — gets the other clock, deferred until it fails. It's the only thing in
the records running both clocks at once, and the damage lands exactly on the
seam.

## The three things that rule out simpler explanations

**Extreme sports rules out him not caring about his body.** From roughly 2000 to
2006 he was totally absorbed in aggressive inline skating and terrain-park
skiing — four summers at Camp Woodward, trampoline training as off-season ski
conditioning, an identity built on what his body could be made to do. Somebody
indifferent to his body doesn't spend six years training one. What's missing
isn't attention or investment. It's upkeep. Training is still configuration —
aimed at a performance ceiling — so it belongs on the input side with everything
else.

**The pets rule out a general fatalism about bodies.** The animals are the one
protected category in his whole archive, and the 2023 message proves it reaches
their physical care: *"I just ran out of the apartment to get him to a doctor
asap."* Immediate, no hedging. The capacity for urgent care about a body is fully
intact. It's just never pointed inward.

**The 2017 dental episode is a check on this page's own strength.** It's the
reason the claim here is *deferred and reactive* rather than *absent*. That's what
it looks like when a falsifier lands partially: the rule survives, narrowed, and
the narrowing gets stated rather than argued away.

## What would prove this wrong

1. **A preventive act.** Any documented case of him starting care *before*
   symptoms — a screening, a physical, a cleaning, a baseline test — with no damage
   already visible. One clean case breaks the missing-setting claim outright.
2. **A dose changed for a health reason.** His cocaine use rose and fell with an
   inheritance and with nothing else. A reduction or substitution made because of
   a physical effect rather than a budget would show upkeep operating inside the
   specification habit.
3. **A medical conversation that isn't paperwork.** A discussion with the
   prescriber about a dose, a taper, or his body — rather than which pharmacy will
   fill the script — breaks the "supplier" reading of that relationship.
4. **Watching that leads to fixing.** If a source shows a monitoring result
   leading to a repair rather than to more monitoring, then the two-setting
   structure is wrong and there's a third one that's simply under-recorded.

## What this page expects to happen

1. **The next physical failure will be absorbed rather than treated**, and will
   turn into an appearance worry within weeks. The teeth are the template: damage
   builds silently, becomes visible, then gets narrated as an image problem
   instead of a medical one.
2. **The Suboxone supply will fail again through geography, not through Tom.**
   The real fragility is an out-of-state prescription meeting a pharmacy that
   won't fill it. Any future withdrawal episode should have a pharmacy or a state
   line at the root of it, with the dealer network as the recovery path.
3. **Resolving his housing won't produce any health change.** As of August 2026
   the living situation is unsettled. Under this rule, stabilising it frees up
   resources for better inputs — not for upkeep, because there's no faculty for
   upkeep to route to.
4. **Any future "health kick" will be an input regime** — a diet, a stack change,
   a substance protocol. Never a course of treatment, physiotherapy, or a
   follow-up schedule. The *form* is more predictable than the content.

## What we still don't know

- **The word count was one pass with a fixed list of terms.** Related
  vocabulary — *urgent care, clinic, X-ray, scan, specialist, therapist, meds* —
  wasn't counted. The archive covers 2015–2025 with **2022 and 2026 missing
  entirely**, so none of this speaks to the present. A wider second pass would
  either put the claim on much firmer ground or break it.
- **No actual medical records exist in the archive at all.** Everything above is
  inferred from what he said about care in text messages, which is a proxy for
  care and not a record of it. A single insurance statement or pharmacy history
  would outrank the whole count.
- **Nothing here dates the bulimia.** Whether the adult food rule grew out of the
  teenage eating disorder or ran alongside it is the single most important
  unknown for this page, because it decides whether specifying is his primary
  habit or a leftover from something else.
- **The extreme-sports material rests on one later recollection**, from 2026, with
  nothing written at the time. If that memory overstates the intensity, that check
  gets weaker.
- **The prescriber is unnamed, and unrecorded before 2019.** Whether the Suboxone
  has been continuously prescribed since 2010, or has moved between prescribed
  and unprescribed stretches, is unresolved — and that changes what the sixteen-year
  "zero relapses" figure is actually measuring.
