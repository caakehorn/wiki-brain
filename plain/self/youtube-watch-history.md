# YouTube Watch History (2007–2026)

**The short version.** Nearly twenty years of Dan's YouTube activity, logged
automatically. It's one of the few honest records of what he actually paid
attention to, rather than what he says he paid attention to. It shows two big
peaks — shock-radio archives in 2012–13, and left-wing political commentary from
2022 on. It also contains two traps that make the raw numbers lie, and both are
explained below.

## Read this before trusting any number here

Two problems undercut the headline figures, and neither is small.

**First: a lot of the "watches" are adverts.** The original export logged 15,149
entries as "Watched" without ever separating real videos from served ad
impressions. A careful re-count found **14.1% of them are Google Ads** — meaning
the number of videos genuinely watched is closer to **13,011**, not the 17,302
the summary reports. Every per-year and per-channel table below counts logged
blocks, not filtered real watches. Treat the volume figures as a ceiling on real
attention, not the attention itself.

**Second: this is only one of his accounts.** Dan confirmed that several YouTube
accounts share the same Google login, and this export covers only one of them.
**Every number on this page is a floor, not a census.** True viewing across all
his accounts is confirmed to be higher than anything here. Until a combined
export exists, no figure on this page is a verified total — including the
often-quoted rate of about 11.58 watches per active day.

## The hundred-day blackout that turned out not to be one

Between **8 November 2025 and 15 February 2026**, this account logged exactly
**one** real video: "Man in Cave (Reupload)," on 8 November itself. The other
**78** logged events in that window are all ad impressions, often in dense
back-to-back runs — about **40 consecutive ads on 4 January 2026 across three and
a half hours**, and another cluster of about **15 on 26 December 2025**.

That looked like a dramatic behavioural collapse, and it isn't. Dan confirmed he
was almost certainly watching on one of his other accounts during that stretch.
The ad-only clusters fit that exactly: a device still logged in and passively
serving ads while the real viewing happened somewhere this export can't see.

The window's timing is striking even though the gap isn't real. It sits inside
the most intense stretch of a relationship crisis documented elsewhere in these
records — the **December 2025 peak of 4,657 messages**, and the sharp imbalance
in engagement through January and February 2026 that followed it.

Real watching returns gradually afterwards: **9 videos in March 2026**, then a
clear rebound — **243 in April, 136 in May**.

## The second trap: the 2026 "explosion" is mostly adverts

Raw counts for mid-2026 suggest an enormous surge — **1,036 events in June** and
**1,616 in the first twenty days of July**. Almost all of it is noise. **80.3% of
June's entries and 96.4% of July's are ads.** Actual videos watched: **204 in
June, and just 58 in the first twenty days of July.**

**The early years are a back catalogue, not a habit.** Fan-run archive channels
devoted to the Opie & Anthony radio universe take the top two places outright —
**168** and **165** watches, with a third archive channel at **115** — sitting
above every music or politics channel in a two-decade log. That's the signature
of someone working through an archive to its end, rather than returning to a
favourite, which is why 2012–13 reads as **exhausting a collection** rather than
as ordinary fandom.

**The later years are politics.** From 2022 the diet is dominated by left-wing
online commentary: Vaush (**177 watches in 2023 alone**), The Majority Report,
David Pakman (**39 in 2022**), Breaking Points (**38 in 2025**), HasanAbi, Some
More News. Alongside it, history and civics — Mr. Beat at **98 in 2023**. Across
all titles, **Trump appears 301 times** and **election 61 times**.

**Music moved to a separate platform.** YouTube Music accounts for about **2,370
watches**, almost entirely from 2022 onward: **691 in 2022, 390 in 2023, 604 in
2024, 987 in 2025.**

## The most recent year, adverts stripped out

Real watches only, August 2025 to July 2026: Breaking Points (112),
LastWeekTonight (95), The Majority Report (93), Tor's Cabinet of Curiosities
(91), We're In Hell (42), JREG (23), Tucker Carlson (22), STRANGE ÆONS (22),
exurb1a (19), DJ Peach Cobbler (17), HasanAbi (16), Chapo Trap House (13).

The political spine is unchanged from 2022 onward. Six of those names are new to
these records. And there's one small human detail: a late-night cluster on **30
June 2026** of 2000s pop-punk nostalgia — Green Day and Fall Out Boy "AOL
Sessions" live videos.

## What the record is good for

This is one of the few genuinely objective long-run measurements available about
Dan. It doesn't depend on him remembering or reporting anything. It tracks a
shift in where he got his information — from pre-social-media shock radio in
2011–14, through the 2020s explosion of online political video — and it lines up
with other records: his Facebook likes from 2012–14 (**196 pages liked in
total**, including ODESZA on 17 October 2014), his political posting elsewhere,
and the periods when he was living in Pennsylvania versus New York.

One consistent thread across every era is a preference for **long, conversational
formats** — hours-long radio archives early, long-form commentary later.

The 2022-onward political viewing isn't where his politics came from. The message
record dates that shift precisely to August 2020. What the watch history shows is
that it **stuck**.

## What we still don't know

- **Every figure here is a floor.** Multiple accounts share one login and only one
  is covered. No total on this page is a real total.
- **The ad problem affects the historical tables too.** Only the recent months
  have been split into real watches versus ads. The year-by-year counts above
  have not been cleaned the same way, so they overstate real viewing by an
  unknown amount — likely somewhere near the 14.1% measured in the old export.
- **Many entries have no channel attached.** Only about **8,728** of the parsed
  entries carry channel information, so the channel rankings are drawn from about
  half the record.
- **A small export inconsistency exists.** 240 URLs present in the older export
  near its July 2025 cutoff don't appear in the newer one. Every sampled case was
  an ad impression from the same three-day boundary window, so Google's ad logging
  simply isn't perfectly repeatable between exports. It affects a handful of ad
  rows, not real watch data.
- **Whether mid-2026 viewing is above or below his true baseline is now an open
  question**, not a settled comparison — because the baseline itself was measured
  on a single account.
