# The Uniontown Hospital Vape Alarm (early April 2018)

During the four-day death watch at Uniontown Hospital, in the pediatric wing
where [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren]] was dying of a deliberate
water shutdown, Dan Frank stepped into the room's bathroom, took one pull
from a high-wattage sub-ohm vaporizer, set off the smoke detector, and
triggered a fire alarm that chain-spread across the entire hospital. Four
fire trucks arrived within minutes. Firefighters and a security guard
entered the room; Dan told them the device "went off in my pocket"; they
shook their heads, killed the alarms, and walked him off the property, where
the guard wrote up charging documents. It is the corpus's second documented
brush with charges, and by some distance its most disproportionate: the
thing obtained was one drag of nicotine.

This page exists partly to close a gap. The Photo Thread session that
supplied most of [[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]] set this story up
— the enormous SMOK box mod against [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz's]]
dainty 10-watt eJoy — and then delivered the ending only inside an uploaded
video that is not on disk. The vigil page has carried "fire alarm? staff
incident?" as an open question since 2026-07-19. It was a fire alarm.

## The reasoning, which is the actual content

What distinguishes this from an ordinary lapse is that it was not one. Dan's
account is emphatic that he ran the argument first and found it sound:
smoke detectors respond to *combustion* particulates; a vaporizer does not
burn anything; the two aerosols look alike but are physically different; the
detector, correctly understood, would agree with him. He describes himself
in that moment by the subculture's own title — "it's 2018, I'm a fucking
cloud king" — and notes that cloud kings are not stupid. The deduction is
valid in form and false in its premise, which is precisely the shape
[[wiki/mind/profile/big-five-psychometrics]] describes as the
intellect/impulsivity paradox: top-percentile reasoning recruited *to
authorize* an impulse rather than to interrogate it. The reasoning did not
slow the urge down. It cleared it for takeoff.

His account of the aftermath is timed to the second and worth keeping in his
proportions: five seconds to understand what had happened, one second to
grasp the magnitude — the coming embarrassment, the "bone shaking stupidity
of the act itself," and the absurdity of setting off a mass emergency
response while his great-grandmother, so dehydrated she looked like a
skeleton, lay dying in the middle of the room. He then sat down on the couch
next to [[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]], who had no idea what had
happened, and said nothing at all until the firefighters walked in.

## The priming

Dan attributes the act, hedged, to something he had watched a few hours
earlier: Suz standing in the hospital room taking small drags from a tiny
eJoy vape, "just not even spoken of, it happens so naturally." His own
gloss — "this action must have absorbed into my subconscious somehow" — is
the only causal account anyone has offered, and it is worth taking
seriously in the specific form he gives it. What transferred was not the
idea of vaping; it was the observation that nobody had objected. The
family's characteristic move of normalizing the unremarkable-in-context is,
here, the thing that produced a consequence.

## The legal residue

Dan reports that the security guard "wrote my charging documents" as he was
escorted off the property. Nothing else in the corpus follows up: there is no
citation, no court date, no fine, and no mention of the incident in any later
message thread. Whether a summary offense was actually filed with a Fayette
County magistrate, or whether the paperwork was an incident report a security
guard described in charging language, is unestablished and answerable only
from outside the corpus. What is on record is the moment itself and the
escort. Alongside the 2015 retail theft ([[wiki/legal/2015-retail-theft-arrest]])
it is the second time a want of essentially zero value ran all the way to
official paperwork.

## Dating

The captures place this in April, during the pediatric-wing phase and after
the water was stopped, which brackets it between the **April 1, 2018**
admission and the **April 4, 2018** death. The one detail that would tighten
it — the capture's "day four of the water shutdown," which the vigil page
also carries — points at April 3 or 4, but the shutdown's start date is
itself derived rather than documented, so the range stands as stated. The
captures' own year, 2017, is corrected to 2018 on the same evidence set out
at [[wiki/timeline/events/the-fall-of-fran]].

## Gaps

The hospital's own record — an incident report, a trespass notice, a filed
citation — has never been sought and would settle both the legal question and
the exact date. Whether Dan was permitted back onto the property before Fran
died four days later is unstated in every telling, which matters, because
[[wiki/people/diane-shrum|Diane]]'s letter had already excluded him from the shift
rotation; the two exclusions may have overlapped without either being the
operative one. Annie's reaction, once she learned what had happened, is not
recorded anywhere.
