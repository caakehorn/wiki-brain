# Operator Testimony Veracity

**This page is evidence, not a claim.** It is the standing state of `testimony/events.jsonl` — every first-person assertion the wiki has recorded, what settled it, and the arithmetic over the settled ones. It states no finding. A finding drawn from this ledger reaches an ordinary page through the normal operations; this one is regenerated by `bin/wiki-testimony page` and overwritten on every write.

## The two numbers

A single trust score would collapse two facts that behave differently. **Veracity** is how often he turns out to be right. **Calibration** is whether his own stated confidence tracks that. The first says how much to believe him; only the second makes an *unproven* claim assessable, because an unproven claim offers nothing to check except the class it belongs to and the confidence it arrived with.

| **Calibration** | Brier 0.334, skill -0.34 vs a coin flip | 16 |

Points are `weight x (2v - 1)`: a confirmed claim earns its full weight, a refuted one loses it, a partial is a wash. Weight is specificity (1-3) times 1.5 where other pages reason from the claim. **Read every figure with its n** — this ledger holds 16 settled claims, not thousands.

## What this ledger cannot see

**The sample is filtered, and not at random.** `CLAUDE.md` carries a standing directive stopping new writing about one living person. `bin/wiki-testimony` enforces it mechanically rather than leaving it to a session to remember, and refuses such a record outright. At least one cleanly adjudicated confirmation is excluded by that rule. The exclusion is correct and it is still a bias: this score is drawn from what the directive permits, not from everything the corpus knows.

**Adjudicated claims are not a random sample of claims.** A claim gets checked when somebody had a reason to check it, and the reasons correlate with the claim being surprising, load-bearing, or already doubted. Expect the settled set to over-represent both the spectacular confirmations and the spectacular failures.

**Nothing here measures honesty.** Every outcome in this ledger is consistent with a person reporting in good faith and misremembering. The failure-mode column exists precisely so the two can be told apart in principle — a distribution dominated by `compression` and `rounding` is a memory, one dominated by `confabulation` running `flattering` would be something else — and at this n it can be told apart in principle only.
