# The Alias Is a Clock, Not a Style

Dan has released music under four names in thirteen years, and the renaming
has never once corresponded to a change in the music. Across SLOPPP, MOGZART
and GRIPNOTIC the two things that would actually constitute a musical
identity — the involuntary **63–85% sub-bass band** and the **complete absence
of sung original lyrics** — are constant and unbroken. What changes is the
name, and the name changes at the boundaries of life periods. The alias is
therefore not doing musical work. It is doing temporal work: it is how a
period gets marked as closed. **The catalog is one continuous body of sound
wearing four different dates.**

This matters because the alias pages, read individually, each present the
rename as an artistic progression — SLOPPP "exploratory," MOGZART "more
refined," GRIPNOTIC "harder-edged, intentional." Read across each other, that
progression dissolves. Every page independently reports the same sub-bass
measurement and the same remix-only output; the stylistic differentiators are
adjectives, while the invariants are numbers. When the descriptions disagree
with the measurements, the measurements are the identity.

## The rule, stated so it can be broken

> Every alias change in the catalog coincides with a life-period boundary,
> and no alias change coincides with a change in either invariant (sub-bass
> 63–85%, no sung original lyrics).

Three things would falsify it, and each is checkable against `raw/`:

1. An alias whose measured sub-bass band falls outside 63–85%.
2. Any release under any alias containing a sung original lyric.
3. A rename that lands cleanly inside a stable life period with no
   corresponding transition — a rebrand that is only a rebrand.

## What the clock reads

The strongest single instance is MOGZART, and it is strong because the name
is used twice. SLOPPP closes with a track literally titled "Goodbye Demo" on
**December 26, 2015** — one month into the Annie relationship — and MOGZART
takes over. MOGZART is then revived on **March 4, 2026**, inside the collapse
that ends that relationship on June 1. The same name opens and closes the same
period. If the alias were a style, a 2026 revival of a 2014 trap-adjacent
project would be a regression; as a clock it is exact.

GRIPNOTIC shows the function outgrowing its medium. It holds the invariants
through the 2015–2019 basement years and into the present, and then on **April
7, 2026** the name is claimed for an AI agent. The naming apparatus is no
longer attached to records at all — it now marks whatever the current project
of self is. That is the same mechanism, applied to a different substrate, and
it is the reason this page sits in `mind` rather than in `interests`.

## The speculation this licenses

If renaming marks time rather than sound, then the aliases are a **legible
periodization of the life, produced without intending to be one** — a
self-generated timeline that is more reliable than a remembered one, because
it was never constructed as testimony. Each name is a bracket someone closed
in real time, for reasons that felt musical and were not.

The load-bearing consequence: an alias boundary is admissible evidence for
dating a life transition, independent of whatever narrative surrounds that
transition. Where the message record and the alias record disagree about when
a period ended, the alias record was not written to persuade anyone.

## Gaps

- **The fourth name is not examined here.** `wiki/interests/music/aliases/mogged-up`
  is outside this page's `synthesizes:` list and its dates were not checked
  against the rule. If it breaks the pattern, this page is wrong and should be
  revised, not deleted.
- **The handoffs are fuzzy.** MOGZART's recorded start (~2014) overlaps
  SLOPPP's run to December 2015. The rule claims coincidence with period
  boundaries, not instantaneous succession, and the overlap is unresolved.
- **The 63–85% figure is a single provenance.** It traces to the
  context-core measurement and is repeated by the alias pages rather than
  independently re-measured per alias. The invariant is asserted three times
  from one source, which is weaker than three measurements.
