Cocaine is the primary daily stimulant in Dan's engineered neurochemical
system — the "cognitive accelerant" he has used, by his own account, for
roughly twenty years, and the substance around which the largest and most
enduring procurement architecture in his life has been built. It is not the
dependency that defines him (that is Suboxone, the structural base), but it
is the one he has spent the most money on, fought the hardest to secure, and
built the most elaborate social infrastructure to deliver. Framed in the
corpus as a tool rather than a vice — "voltage," "the accelerator," "CPU &
RAM" atop a Suboxone "chassis" — it sits at the center of the three
domains the wiki tracks most intensively: the chemical architecture of his
body, the supply network of his relationships, and the legal and family
history that shaped both.

## Genesis: family exposure, and first use

Cocaine first entered Dan's household as a known concept at **age 13
(2001)**, through the discovery of his parents' own use — the same
substance later implicated in the November 2005 rupture was already a
familiar, "adult" behavior in the house years before that hinge event.
His father's rehab admission and his mother's affair with their cocaine
dealer both sit inside a broader pattern of alcohol-adjacent family
dysfunction in which cocaine was present but not, for Dan, radioactive.
The selectivity is the diagnostic detail: four family members across
three generations were functional alcoholics, and alcohol is specifically
the substance present at the household's defining betrayals. Dan excluded
alcohol permanently and adopted cocaine freely — abstinence as
anti-identification with the family's mechanism of betrayal, not with
substance use itself.

His own first cocaine use followed at **17–18 (2005–2006)**, in the
[[wiki/places/seven-springs|Seven Springs]] ski-crew context, and a
later self-audit explicitly frames the choice as instrumental as much as
social: cocaine as a chemical means of weight control tied to the
adolescent bulimia documented on
[[wiki/timeline/periods/dark-era-2007-2008|the dark-era page]], alongside
its value as a subcultural credential inside the ski/band scene.

Cocaine use stopped entirely during the **2007–2008 opiate-pivot window**
— the period when physical opiate dependence became undeniable (a
November 2007 Las Vegas trip) and a single heroin attempt was rejected
on aesthetic grounds ("felt dirty"). It resumed in **2012**, two years
after the Suboxone stabilization, as a deliberate "voltage" layer added
on top of an already-secure base rather than a relapse.

## The dosage arc

The clearest single quantified fact about cocaine in the corpus is a
**dosage arc tied directly to Dan's finances** rather than to any stated
desire to moderate:

| Period | Daily quantity | Driver |
|---|---|---|
| ~2006–2016 (baseline) | ~1g/day | Pre-inheritance constraint |
| 2017–2020 (peak) | **3.5–7g/day** | Fran's inheritance removed the capital constraint |
| 2020–present (contraction) | **~0.5–1g/day** | Inheritance exhausted |

The retrospective's own reading is blunt: the escalation shows the
"desired state" is **maximum intensity limited only by supply and money,
not by any internal ceiling** — and the later reduction is framed
explicitly as a **resource adjustment, not a step toward moderation or
recovery**. The daily-cocaine baseline sits atop a 96th-percentile
impulsiveness score in the deviance audit.

## Role in the chemical stack

Within the stack documented on
[[wiki/health/chemical-architecture|chemical-architecture]], cocaine's
role is explicitly computational:

| Substance | Metaphor | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Suboxone | Chassis & OS | System stabilizer (daily since 2010-02-17) |
| **Cocaine** | **CPU & RAM** | **Cognitive & drive accelerator** |
| Nicotine | Thermal paste | Ritualistic regulator |
| Cannabis | Creative lubricant | Daily use |
| Alcohol | — | Zero (family-pattern exclusion) |

The framing is engineering: cocaine as a component "tuned to maintain
extreme cognitive and existential intensity without succumbing to total
chaos." The wiki records that frame as the documented self-model while
flagging what it elides: the withdrawal vulnerability is real (May 2026
demonstrated the system fails hard when one supplier flakes), and the
framing itself is load-bearing identity — "the addiction is the operating
system," which is exactly what an addiction would say.

## Family context

Cocaine is the substance most tightly woven into the Frank family's
rupture narrative. The November 2005 divorce sits at the center: Dan's
father Rick admitted to rehab, his mother Suzanne revealed to have been
affairing with their cocaine dealer. The cocaine is not incidental to
the rupture — it is the substance that connected the two betrayals
(her affair, his addiction) to the same source. Yet Dan's own later use
carries no equivalent charge; it was adopted freely and built into a
maintained, defended architecture. The exclusion is alcohol, not
stimulants — a pattern read on
[[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suzanne's page]] as abstinence coded to the
exact family mechanism of betrayal rather than to substance use itself.

Suzanne also served as Dan's **earliest documented cocaine provision
node** — delivering cocaine to Dan and Annie at 05:02 on 2015-11-30,
with a car made conditional on completing the eviction, and supplying
them both routinely through 2015–16. The architecture claim is
unaffected and the redundancy claim gains a node: the network had a
family-internal source available before any of the named dealers.

## Legal history

Dan's only real arrest was for cocaine. On **February 17, 2015**, at
roughly 3am in downtown Uniontown, he was pulled over by Pennsylvania
State Police returning from delivering lemons to his mother at a pool
hall, driving [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran's]] disused car with an
expired registration and bald tires. The car was impounded; the impound
produced an inventory search; the inventory search produced three
charges. The load-bearing one: **possession of a controlled substance
for the residue in an empty cocaine bag** — a Class B count for a bag
corner saved for a 10% bump.

The arrest triggered a DA policy that denied ARD to anyone charged with
a cocaine misdemeanor, residue or not. Dan's lawyer defeated the
categorical bar, and ARD was granted in February 2016 — keeping his
record clean. The diagnostic detail, per
[[wiki/mind/concepts/acquisition-drive|acquisition-drive]], is that
the drive was running on almost nothing: a bag corner and one hitter's
worth of weed, carried on a 3am errand for no reason, produced a
felony-adjacent charge out of the smallest quantity in the record.

## The supply chain

Cocaine is the primary product moving through the supply network across
its entire documented history. Every node in
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/supply-network|the network]] either supplied
cocaine explicitly or operated in a context where cocaine was the
dominant unnamed product. The succession of cocaine-specific nodes:

| Node | Era | Role | Product specificity |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suzanne Frank]] | 2015–16 | Family-internal provision node | Explicit cocaine, routine provision |
| [[wiki/people/lindsay-carolla|Lindsay Carolla]] | 2016 (Thanksgiving) | Small-scale peer | Explicit cocaine ("Hi honey," "yes my love") |
| [[wiki/people/jay-lauer|Jay Lauer]] | 2016–17 | Peer trader (mutual credit) | Cocaine + suboxone trading; died of heroin overdose |
| [[wiki/people/johnny-dealer|Johnny]] | 2018–19 | Deep-cycle primary dealer | "B's" (balls/cocaine), suboxone strips; first supply panics |
| [[wiki/people/al|Al]] ("Socialist") | 2018 | Parallel Uniontown dealer | Explicit cocaine; ends in unpaid-debt confrontation |
| [[wiki/people/annoying|Annoying]] (unidentified) | 2019 | Short-term NYC dealer | Explicit cocaine; $300/ball, $8ball/280 |
| [[wiki/people/menore|Menore]] | 2018–25 (continuous) | NYC professional delivery | Never named in-thread — 0 product terms in 4,413 messages; **"bags," and explicitly not weed**, when named outside it |
| [[wiki/people/tom|Tom]] | ~2010–2026 | Friend-supplier (strips, cocaine, mushrooms, klonopin) | Cocaine among products; sole strip source by 2025 |
| **Bop** | 2026–present | Current primary node (daily house calls at noon) | Unnamed; five handoffs in six days documented July–Aug 2026 |

The cocaine supply chain demonstrates the network's characteristic
reliability inversion: the more purely transactional the relationship,
the more reliable the supply. Menore (professional, unnamed product)
answered 455 of 458 requests across 6.5 years. Al (transactional,
explicit cocaine) cut contact over an unpaid debt. Tom (friendship-embedded,
multi-product) ran the owe-and-silence cycle twice — in 2014 and again
in 2026 — and his May 2026 no-show left Dan working a register in
withdrawal.

## Relationship dynamics

Cocaine procurement is the load-bearing mechanism of the
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] relationship's final year. In the
terminal phase (August 2025 – March 2026), Dan is no longer only the
network's consumer — he is its distribution point for Annie, who is
unemployed, dependent, and sourcing through him ("bop"). The supply
architecture, not sentiment, is the load-bearing structure of the
relationship's final year.

Dan states the procurement-as-access thesis in his own voice on the
night of July 28, 2026, mid-argument, unprompted and against his own
position:

> *"even the worst thing I've done — getting you drugs — didn't really
> benefit me nearly as much as it was about that being the only way you
> would see me."*

The thesis is inferred from the record on
[[wiki/mind/synthesis/dan-annie-fallout-verdict|the fallout verdict]]
page; it is now also testimony.

An earlier cocaine-specific exchange — the **$200 cocaine spend on
2015-12-29** — sits unresolved in the corpus and is flagged on
[[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suzanne's page]]: Annie says *"And that
$200 wasn't even your own"* (23:48); Dan answers *"It was my money"*
(23:50). The two lines conflict and the corpus does not settle them.
The exchange is analytically useful regardless: the couple is arguing
about the ownership of cocaine money within weeks of the relationship's
start, which is the procurement-as-retention mechanism visible in
embryo.

## Financial footprint

The cocaine dosage arc is the single best-documented case in the corpus
of **money-as-the-independent-variable** in substance consumption:
the 2017–2020 escalation to 3.5–7g daily tracks Fran's inheritance
arrival with precision, and the post-2020 contraction to 0.5–1g tracks
its exhaustion. The retrospective is explicit that the reduction is a
resource adjustment — a budget response, not a moderation step.

The network's financial fragility is documented on the
[[wiki/people/jay-lauer|Jay Lauer]] page: the peer-trading tier ran on
$10 and $20 fronting requests across the 2016–17 winter, and the same
seven-week window of mutual credit between broke men is the window Jay
did not come out of. The cocaine economy's participants were as
financially fragile as its buyer — precarity ran the whole chain.

## What's missing

- **Prescriber information and medical oversight**: No prescriber, clinic,
  or medical record for the cocaine use appears anywhere in the corpus —
  it is entirely self-directed and undocumented by any clinical source.
- **Post-2026 dosage state**: The dosage arc is documented through the
  inheritance exhaustion (~2020 onward), but no figure is on record for
  the 2026–present window, when Bop replaced Tom as the daily node.
- **The cocaine–bulimia link** is stated in a self-audit but never
  independently corroborated by any source outside Dan's own retrospective
  account.
- **Product specificity in the NYC era**: Menore's product is still not
  positively identified, but the question is narrower than it was — see
  the re-check below. It is *not* weed, it is sold in **bags**, and the
  thread's silence on the point is now measured rather than impressionistic.
- **The true scale of the 2017–2020 spend**: The dosage arc gives
  quantities but not dollar figures; the financial cost of the 3.5–7g
  daily peak is undocumented.
- **Post-overdose intervention**: Dan's attempt to move Jay Lauer onto
  Suboxone — the only documented instance of Dan trying to move somebody
  *out* of this network rather than into or around it — is recorded, but
  whether Dan made any similar attempt with any other node (before or
  after) is not on record.
