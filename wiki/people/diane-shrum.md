# Diane (Rebecca Diane Van Voorhis Shrum)

Diane is Dan's maternal grandmother, [[wiki/people/suzanne-frank|Suz's]]
mother, and — the genealogical record now confirms — the only documented
child of [[wiki/people/fran-coldren|Fran Coldren]]. She appears in the corpus
almost entirely as an antagonist, and at one specific moment: in April 2018,
while Fran was dying at Uniontown Hospital, Dan and
[[wiki/people/annie-ulmer|Annie]] each received a letter informing them that
they were not to be in Fran's house unsupervised and could not visit Fran
without a family-assigned monitor and requests submitted in advance. Dan's
account of the letters is unprintably hostile and entirely unhedged, and his
objection is not that they inconvenienced him — it is that "nothing would
have made Fran more upset and furious than this."

## Who she is in the line

The 23andMe genealogy export settles a descent the wiki previously had wrong.
Fran married three times; her only recorded child came from the first
marriage.

|---|---|---|

| Their daughter | **Rebecca Diane Van Voorhis** | 1939-01-30, West Virginia |
| Diane's husband | George Dixon Shrum Jr. | 1937-06-14, Pittsburgh |
| Their daughter | Suzanne Whyel Shrum | 1962-09-15, Pittsburgh |

The April 1940 census places Emmet, Fran and the fourteen-month-old Diane
together in Smithfield, Fayette County — Fran's arrival in the county that
becomes the family's terminus. The same record set shows Diane living in
**Farmington Hills, Michigan between 1985 and 2010**, which is the only
geographic fact the corpus supplies about her adult life and which would put
her out of state for most of Dan's childhood. Nothing in the message corpus
contradicts or confirms that.

## What she did, and why it lands the way it does

The letters were sent before the final hospitalization, not in response to
it — Dan is explicit that "they sent the letter well before this happened."
That timing matters. It was not a reaction to a crisis; it was a standing
position on who was permitted access to a 97-year-old woman and her house,
issued while Dan and Annie were the people executing her stated wish to stay
in it.

The consequence was concrete. When Judge [[wiki/people/fred-adams|Fred
Adams]] mandated that someone be with Fran twenty-four hours a day in the
pediatric wing, shifts were assigned — and Dan and Annie were not given any.
Dan's own note on this is characteristically flat: "I wasn't really
complaining." They went anyway, on their own, and were the two people in the
room when she died ([[wiki/timeline/events/fran-death-vigil]]). The
exclusion order and the actual deathbed presence are the same story told
twice: the family that held the authority did not attend, and the family
members stripped of authority did.

There is a second, quieter consequence. In August 2020, negotiating his
$144,069.31 estate distribution, Dan asked the estate attorney directly what
the odds were that his grandmother would contest it. The wiki has carried
the identity of that grandmother as an open gap. It is almost certainly
Diane — she is the estate's direct heir, she is the grandmother the corpus
otherwise documents, and she had already, two years earlier, put her
objection to Dan's proximity to Fran's assets in writing. The question was
not paranoid.
