# Knowledge Preservation Philosophy

## Purpose

wiki-brain is designed as a personal semantic memory graph rather than a conventional collection of notes or a normalized database.

The goal is not merely to store information currently considered important. The goal is to preserve enough context, detail, relationships, chronology, and ambiguity that future analysis can discover meaning that was not obvious at the moment of capture.

The wiki should behave more like a memory substrate than a database.

Traditional systems optimize for:

- normalization
- minimal redundancy
- immediate usefulness
- structured fields

wiki-brain intentionally optimizes for:

- context preservation
- future inference
- semantic richness
- narrative continuity
- relationship discovery
- longitudinal understanding

## Preserve Before Compressing

The default instinct of information systems is to remove noise. The default instinct of wiki-brain should be to preserve potential signal.

A detail should not be discarded merely because its importance is not obvious today.

Future analysis systems may discover relationships between details that the original author could not anticipate.

Useful details include:

- exact places
- names
- dates
- chronology
- family context
- social relationships
- emotional reactions
- jokes
- embarrassing memories
- contradictions
- uncertainty
- sensory preferences
- mundane routines
- seemingly trivial objects

The details are not clutter. The details are the searchable memory.

## Why Mundane Details Matter

Weak capture:

> Dan liked skiing as a child.

Rich capture:

> Dan received his first Seven Springs season pass before age one. His family spent nearly every winter weekend from December through April skiing. They eventually purchased a ski-in/ski-out condominium in the Villages at Seven Springs. Many childhood friendships formed through that skiing community rather than through school.

The richer version supports future reasoning about:

- childhood socioeconomic environment
- geography
- family traditions
- recreational identity
- friendship formation
- community belonging
- childhood routine
- personal history

## Raw Capture vs Canonical Knowledge

wiki-brain uses a two-layer architecture.

## Raw Layer

The raw layer preserves the original record.

Raw entries are:

- immutable source material
- historical evidence
- the closest representation of original capture
- uncompressed memory

Raw entries should not:

- be rewritten for elegance
- remove embarrassing content
- remove jokes
- remove uncertainty
- remove emotional framing
- be optimized into summaries

Raw is the archaeological artifact.

Example:

```
raw/
2026-08-01_story.md
```

## Canonical Layer

The canonical layer creates useful semantic objects.

Canonical entries may:

- reorganize information
- add structure
- create cross-links
- add metadata
- connect concepts
- summarize patterns

Canonical entries are interpretations built on top of raw records, not replacements for them.

Example:

```
stories/
Bald-Eagle-Cummings.md
```

## Canonical Entry Philosophy

Canonical entries should preserve three layers:

### Events

What happened?

Example: "Dan attended a Seven Springs ski trip during seventh grade."

### Context

What surrounded the event?

Example: "Dan had been skiing at Seven Springs since infancy and considered it a major childhood environment."

### Meaning

Why does this matter?

Example: "The event became associated with adolescent humiliation and social reputation."

All three layers are valuable.

## Entity Creation Philosophy

Entity creation is not automatic extraction. The goal is not that every noun becomes a page.

The goal is to create nodes that improve future retrieval.

Create entities for:

- recurring people
- important relationships
- meaningful places
- schools, workplaces, organizations, and communities

A person or place may begin with only one paragraph. Sparse nodes are acceptable because they provide attachment points for future information.

The graph should grow organically.

Avoid creating pages simply because:

- a noun appears once
- a place name exists
- an object is briefly mentioned

Ask:

> Would future retrieval benefit from having this as a connection point?

## Preserve Uncertainty

Human memories are not databases. Autobiographical records contain imperfect recall, uncertainty, rumors, interpretations, and changing understanding.

Do not flatten uncertainty into false certainty.

Correct:

> Dan recalls that Victor later told him X happened.

Incorrect:

> Victor definitely did X.

## Preserve Subjective Interpretation

The wiki should preserve both:

### Objective layer

What happened?

### Subjective layer

What did this mean to the person?

Subjective interpretation is part of the historical record.

## Emotional Context Is Data

Emotional information should not be sanitized.

Valid preserved information includes:

- shame
- pride
- confusion
- embarrassment
- nostalgia
- resentment
- humor
- affection

These can explain later behavior better than factual summaries alone.

## Contradictions Are Valuable

Do not automatically resolve contradictions.

Contradictions may reveal:

- changing perspectives
- memory evolution
- emotional conflict
- incomplete information

Preserve them.

## Agent Guidance

Future AI systems reading this wiki should:

- preserve details
- maintain chronology
- create intentional cross-links
- distinguish facts from interpretations
- retain uncertainty
- use rich context
- respect raw and canonical layers

They should not:

- summarize away details
- remove embarrassing material
- convert subjective memories into facts
- aggressively compress
- optimize only for short answers
- discard mundane information

## Style Guidance

Entries should favor:

- specificity
- proper nouns
- dates when known
- locations
- relationships
- chronology
- cross-links

Avoid vague summaries.

Prefer:

> Dan attended Laurel Highlands School District, spent winters at Seven Springs, formed friendships through skiing, and remembers specific childhood events that shaped his understanding of social reputation.

Over:

> Dan had a difficult childhood.
