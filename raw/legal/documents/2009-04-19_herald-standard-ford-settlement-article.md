**"Frank's, Ford settle dispute"**, Herald-Standard, April 19, 2009, 1-minute
read, tagged "Local News." Full text:

> A lawsuit that Uniontown-based Frank's Auto Supermarket filed against Ford
> Motor Co. last month has been dismissed because they settled the matter
> out of court. The auto supply company claimed that they've been using the
> catchphrase "We Speak Car" since 2003. The suit alleged that a Ford
> advertising campaign that started in early March uses the phrase to
> promote its 2010 Ford Fusion and Fusion hybrid cars. In an order Friday,
> U.S. District Judge Arthur J. Schwab closed the case. The order notes
> that the parties "agreed to an amicable resolution of this matter."
>
> The order did not include what the resolution entailed, but noted Schwab
> will retain jurisdiction if either side fails to execute the settlement
> agreement.
>
> Frank's Auto, which has sold automobile parts since 1938, has seven
> locations in Pennsylvania, including the original store on Beeson
> Boulevard in Uniontown.
>
> The suit had claimed that the retailer has been using the "We Speak Car"
> slogan in its own ads for six years, and that Ford's use of the
> catchphrase could harm its business.
>
> The suit had asked a judge to order that all of Ford's literature and
> advertising with the slogan on it be destroyed.

Confirms the trademark suit filed March 27, 2009 (Frank's Auto Supply, Inc.
v. Ford Motor Company, Case No. 2:09-cv-365, W.D. Pa.) was resolved: Frank's
Auto Supply/Supermarket and Ford Motor Company **settled the "We Speak
Car" trademark dispute amicably**, out of court, with settlement terms not
publicly disclosed (sealed/confidential per the judge's order, standard for
this kind of quick trademark resolution). The dismissal order was entered
**Friday, April 17, 2009** (the article, published Sunday April 19, refers
to "an order Friday"). Judge Schwab retained jurisdiction in case either
side failed to execute the settlement — no record of that happening.

One new detail not present in the complaint excerpt available (which cuts
off at paragraph 25, before the prayer for relief): **the suit had asked
the court to order all of Ford's "We Speak Car" literature and advertising
destroyed** — an aggressive ask for a $60–80M national ad campaign,
underscoring that this was a real fight, not a token filing, even though it
resolved quickly. Minor discrepancy worth flagging: the article gives the
original store's address as "Beeson Boulevard," while the complaint gives
FAS's principal place of business as "75 South Beeson Avenue" — likely the
same street referred to two ways (journalist shorthand vs. legal filing),
not two different locations.

This closes the "outcome unknown" gap the CATO dossier flagged and the
prior version of `wiki/people/rick-frank.md` carried forward unresolved:
the case did not go to trial or produce a public verdict; it settled
amicably within roughly three weeks of filing.
